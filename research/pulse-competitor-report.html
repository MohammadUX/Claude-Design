Section 1

Summary

Snapping a photo of your food is now standard. What sets apps apart is what happens after the photo: can people trust the estimate, and does the AI change what they do next? No sampled app puts food, training and recovery in one daily plan.

8of 10

Photo logging is table stakes

Eight of the ten core competitors offer AI photo logging. It no longer sets anyone apart. The review step after the photo is where apps differ.

0of 10

None shows how confident the AI is per item

Cal AI asks "How did Cal AI do?" after the fact. MacroFactor notes that its AI "can make mistakes". No app flags which item is the shaky guess, like the oil or the sauce.

1of 10

Only one coach mixes food and training data

Bevel's coach reads a breakfast photo next to cardio load. Other coaches either start with no data (Alma), compare you to population averages (WHOOP), or give generic lists (Noom).

2camps

Food and fitness live in separate apps

Calorie apps don't plan training. Training and recovery apps treat nutrition as an add-on: Garmin's nutrition tab starts with a free-trial prompt. Bevel is the only one in the sample that does both.

The opening for Pulse

Be the app where each photo, workout and night of sleep changes today's plan, and the AI shows its work.

Section 4

Flow-by-flow teardown

For each flow: what competitors do, with links to the screens, and the gap the pattern leaves open.

Daily dashboard

Maps to Pulse › Today
What we saw
  • A calories-remaining ring with three macro bars is used by almost every app: Cal AI, Lifesum, Yazio, MacroFactor.
  • At the start of the day the dashboard reads "0 eaten / 2,041 remaining" and suggests nothing to do (MacroFactor, Yazio).
  • MyFitnessPal repeats the same ring and water widgets twice in one screen.
  • Noom shows a "weight loss zone" band on the calorie bar, the only sampled view that frames the goal as a range rather than a single number.
Gap
Unmet needDashboards report numbers but don't recommend anything. Remaining calories never account for today's workout or last night's sleep.

Lead with one next action (what to eat or do next), and show the target as a range that moves with training load.

AI meal scan and review

Maps to Pulse › AI Meal Scan
What we saw
  • The camera screens are nearly identical: a viewfinder with frame corners and a shutter button (Yazio, MyFitnessPal, Garmin, Oura).
  • The best review screens break the meal into ingredients you can edit: MacroFactor "AI Plate", BitePal (grams per ingredient), Life Reset.
  • Trust signals come after logging, if at all: Cal AI's thumbs rating and "Fix Issue", and MacroFactor's small "AI can make mistakes" line.
  • Life Reset gives 1,394 kcal for one pasta plate and doesn't ask how much butter or cream went in, which is where most of the uncertainty is.
Gap
Unmet needPeople can't tell which number to double-check. Hidden calories (oil, sauce, dressing, drinks) are the biggest error source and nobody prompts for them.

Show confidence per item, point to the single least-certain item, and ask one quick question ("cooked in oil or butter?") before logging.

AI coach

Maps to Pulse › AI Coach
What we saw
  • Alma tells a new user "I don't have any food log data to analyze yet", so the first conversation is generic.
  • WHOOP Coach answers by comparing you to "men ages 30 to 34", which is interesting but gives you nothing to do.
  • Noom replies with a generic list of snacks.
  • Bevel is the strongest: it reads a breakfast photo alongside "Cardio Load: Overtraining" and advises recovery.
  • No sampled coach answer ends with a button that logs, plans or swaps something.
Gap
Unmet needThe coach sits next to your data instead of using it, and its advice is a dead end: you have to go and apply it yourself.

Start every answer from the user's own numbers, end it with one-tap actions, and cover the first day with onboarding answers and imported wearable data.

Workouts and readiness

Maps to Pulse › Workouts
What we saw
  • Readiness scores explain what the number means (Tempo, The Outsiders) but don't change the workout planned for the day.
  • Bevel shows strain, recovery and sleep rings above its workout templates. The two sit side by side and don't affect each other.
  • Strength loggers (Hevy, Gymshark, WHOOP) are set and rep tables with no link to food.
  • The Outsiders surfaces "Overnight Data Missing", a rare honest state for when data didn't sync.
Gap
Unmet needReadiness is reported but doesn't adjust anything. Training doesn't feed back into the food plan, and a workout doesn't raise the protein target.

Let readiness scale the day's workout load and adjust the calorie and protein targets in the same step, and say why.

Progress and trends

Maps to Pulse › Progress
What we saw
  • The best-designed projections appear in onboarding and on paywalls to sell the subscription: Yazio, Lifesum, Cal AI ("great potential to crush your goal").
  • In the app itself, charts are often sparse: Future Pro shows a flat line with one entry; Noom shows four points and a goal line.
  • Bevel's trend analysis is the reference: 3, 7, 14 and 30-day changes plus a 30-day projection based on rolling averages.
Gap
Unmet needCharts show what the scale did but not why: water after leg day, weekend eating, logging gaps. People give up during plateaus that the data could explain.

Explain weekly changes in plain language, point out the pattern behind them, and suggest one experiment to try next week.

Paywall and monetisation

Not yet designed in Pulse
What we saw
  • Cal AI shows the paywall before the user has logged anything ($29.99/yr).
  • Garmin's nutrition tab opens on "Start your Free Trial" with no preview of what you'd get.
  • Alma lists concrete outcomes ("Ask what's actually working in your diet"); Yazio ties the price to a goal date.
Gap
Unmet needUsers pay before the AI has shown it understands them.

Let the first three scans and one weekly summary be free, then put the paywall on the value the user has already seen.

Problems no one has solved

Problems that appeared across the sample and that no competitor fully addresses, ranked by how much they affect whether people keep using the app.

Critical

Estimates people can't judge

AI photo logging gives one confident-looking number. Users can't see which item is a guess, so they either trust wrong totals or give up on the feature.

EvidenceLife ResetCal AI
Critical

Hidden calories go unasked

Cooking oil, butter, dressings and drinks don't show up well in photos. No review step asks about them, so daily totals come out too low in a consistent way.

EvidenceMacroFactorBitePal
Critical

Food and training are planned separately

People who lift or run need their targets to change with their training, but calorie apps and training apps don't talk to each other. Nobody adjusts today's food target from today's readiness.

EvidenceGarminBevel
High

The coach has nothing to say on day one

Coaches that rely on logged data give generic answers in the first week, which is when people decide whether to keep the app.

EvidenceAlmaNoom
High

Advice with no next step

Coach answers end as text. Applying them means leaving the chat and doing it by hand in another part of the app.

EvidenceWHOOPBevel
High

Unexplained plateaus

Weight charts show the line but not the reason. Plateaus and water swings look like failure, and that is when people churn.

EvidenceNoomFuture Pro
Medium

Missed days and logging fatigue

Streaks and "0 eaten" screens penalise gaps. No sampled app offers a quick way to fill in yesterday or a lighter mode for busy weeks.

EvidenceYazioMacroFactor
Medium

No healthy relationship safeguards

Every dashboard centres on calorie numbers. None of the sampled apps offered a mode without numbers or a gentle check when intake stays very low, which matters for people at risk of disordered eating.

Section 8

Method and sources

How this was done

We ran seven searches of Mobbin's iOS library covering six flows: daily dashboard, AI meal scan and its review screen, AI coach chat, workouts and readiness, progress, and paywalls. We looked at every returned screen and compared what it showed. The matrix only records what appeared in these samples; the apps may have more elsewhere. Positions on the market map are the analyst's judgment.

Apps in scope
